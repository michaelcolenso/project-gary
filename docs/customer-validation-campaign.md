# OpportunityForge Customer Validation Campaign

**Purpose:** Find one product worth building by replacing plausibility scores with evidence from real buyers.

**Status:** Draft only. No outreach may be sent until Michael approves the exact recipients and messages.

## Campaign design

Test three materially different hypotheses in parallel:

1. **OSS Radar** — HN discussion × GitHub star velocity × Wikipedia attention
2. **Supplier Risk Lookup** — OSHA violations × EPA enforcement
3. **Labor-Market Reality Check** — BLS employment/wages × Wikipedia/HN attention

Five prospects per hypothesis. The first message does not pitch a product or mention pricing. It asks about a concrete past workflow.

## Evidence hierarchy

| Signal | Points | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| No reply after one follow-up | 0 | No evidence; weak channel or weak problem |
| Polite answer with no example | 1 | Fluff; do not count as validation |
| Describes a specific recent event | 2 | Problem exists |
| Names current tool/process and cost/time | 3 | Existing behavior and budget signal |
| Shares artifact/data or agrees to show workflow | 5 | Meaningful time commitment |
| Introduces budget owner or colleague | 7 | Reputation commitment |
| Agrees to prototype review/pilot | 8 | Strong advancement |
| Pays deposit/preorder | 10 | Commercial validation |

**Advance threshold:** at least 2 of 5 prospects in a segment must reach 5+ points before building an MVP.

**Kill threshold:** after five initial messages and one respectful follow-up, zero specific workflow examples means the hypothesis is paused.

## Initial outreach drafts

### A. OSS Radar — engineering / DevRel / OSPO

**Subject:** Quick question about how you spot durable OSS adoption

Hi {{first_name}} — I’m researching how technical teams distinguish a genuinely durable open-source adoption wave from a brief burst of attention.

Could you tell me about the last time {{company}} evaluated a fast-rising library or developer tool? What signals did you check, and what made the decision difficult?

I’m not selling anything; I’m trying to understand the actual workflow before deciding whether this problem is worth working on. Even a two-line reply would be useful.

— Michael Colenso

### B. Supplier Risk — procurement / responsible sourcing

**Subject:** Question about a recent supplier-risk review

Hi {{first_name}} — I’m researching how procurement teams investigate operational or regulatory risk in private suppliers.

Could you walk me through the last supplier review where workplace-safety or environmental enforcement history mattered? What sources did you check, and where did the process break down or become manual?

I’m not pitching a tool; I’m trying to understand how this is handled today. A short reply would help.

— Michael Colenso

### C. Labor-market intelligence — career/workforce product leaders

**Subject:** How do you separate hiring reality from trend noise?

Hi {{first_name}} — I’m researching how workforce and career-product teams decide whether a fast-growing skill or occupation represents durable labor demand rather than temporary cultural attention.

Could you tell me about the last time your team made that call? Which datasets or indicators did you trust, and what information arrived too late?

I’m not selling anything; I’m trying to understand the real decision process. Even a brief example would be valuable.

— Michael Colenso

## Follow-up after 5–7 days

Hi {{first_name}} — one quick follow-up, then I’ll leave you alone. I’m specifically trying to learn what happened the *last time* you made this decision, including any spreadsheet, dashboard, or manual research involved. If someone else owns this workflow, an introduction would be equally helpful.

— Michael

## Reply interview script

Do not describe the proposed product until the end, if at all.

1. “When did this last come up?”
2. “What triggered the investigation?”
3. “Walk me through exactly what you did.”
4. “Which sources or tools did you use?”
5. “How long did it take, and who else was involved?”
6. “What was the consequence of getting it wrong?”
7. “What have you tried to improve the process?”
8. “Where does budget for this kind of work sit?”
9. “Could you show me the spreadsheet/report/dashboard you used?”
10. Advancement ask: “Could I return with a rough prototype using one of your past cases and get 20 minutes of feedback?”

## Rules

- Never guess an email address.
- Never claim a referral or relationship that does not exist.
- No mass blast; personalize each message to the prospect’s public work.
- No “Would you buy this?” or “Is this a good idea?” questions.
- Separate factual evidence from interpretation.
- Stop after one follow-up unless the prospect engages.
- Michael approves the exact recipient list and rendered messages before sending.
